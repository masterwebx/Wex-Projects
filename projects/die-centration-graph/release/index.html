Trends

The long-range picture: are we missing checks, and which fails keep showing up?

What each chart is telling you

Missed checks by week

A missed check is a posted item-day with no quality measurement. Each point is one week. The line going up means more holes that week. When Line is “All lines”, you get one colored line per machine — hover a line in the legend to see which machine it is. A flat zero line is the goal.

Fails by week

Each bar is one week. Colors stack, so a tall bar that is half red and half blue means two different fail reasons that week. Typical reasons: thickness over, thickness under, density under, density over, range over, cell count. Use the Fail type dropdown to isolate one reason.

Quality fails by item # / MSPEC

How many quality fails each item or MSPEC has in the current filters. Click a bar to open History for that item or MSPEC (fails in the date range). Item # and MSPEC lists only include products that have fails for the Fail type you picked.

Top quality fails

A Pareto chart: the longest bar is the most common fail. Fix that first — it is where the most defects sit. The number on the right is how many times that fail appeared in the current filters.

Summary cards

  • Avg missed / week — typical weekly holes. Compare after you change the date range.
  • Highest fail week — the worst week in range.
  • Most common fail — same as the top Pareto bar, in words.
  • Repeat fail items — items that failed at least twice. Those are chronic, not one-offs.
  • Chronic lines — machines with the highest share of missed checks.

From defaults to the first day of last month so you land on a useful window instead of the entire history.

MSPECs

The Master Sheet limits that came in with Copy for Graph. Foam only.

If a spec looks wrong on the graph, check this page first. You can still type over specs on the graph for this session; the Master Sheet row is what came from Excel.

SPC charts

SPC means Statistical Process Control: the same measurement, plotted over time, so you can see drift before a lot fails. Foam uses thickness, range, and density. Extrusion Bubble and Garland Bubble plot Basis Weight. Not used for P1 or RTS.

The controls on the left

  • View by Item # or MSPEC — Item # is one product. MSPEC groups every item that shares that spec. Garland Bubble can also view by Structure.
  • The list is sorted most points first, so the product you run the most is at the top. Each row shows how many checks (pts) it has in the date range.
  • From / To — From starts on the first day of last month. Change it if you need a longer look.
  • Average / range / density — foam summary charts. Average is mean thickness, Range is high-minus-low across the die (in thousandths), Density is pcf.
  • Basis Weight — Bubble and Garland chart. Garland draws the BW Target from the check sheet. Visalia Bubble plots the Weight column.
  • Each T point over time — one chart per die location (T1, T2, …). Scroll if there are many. Use this when one side of the die is drifting.
  • Hide / Show outliers — points way outside the spec window are hidden by default so one bad type-in does not squash the scale.
  • Click a dot, then use Centration graph in the tools bar to open that exact check. The trail becomes Home › SPC › Graph.
  • Series Centration Graph overlays every die check in the date range on one radial map — see the Series graph topic.

Hover any point on the live chart for date, value, and which check it is. CPK numbers on the right are explained in the CPK stats topic.

CPK stats

These numbers answer: “Is this process tight enough, and is it aimed at the middle of the spec?” They update for whichever Item / MSPEC and date range you have on the SPC page.

n count

How many measurements are in the current chart (after hidden outliers, if they are hidden). You need at least 2 to compute the rest. More points = a more trustworthy Cpk. A Cpk from 8 points is a sketch; a Cpk from 80 is a real signal.

Mean average

Add every value, divide by n. This is the center of the bell. Compare it to Target. If mean sits near Max, the process is running thick even if most points still pass.

Stdev spread

Sample standard deviation (the usual “n − 1” formula). It is how far a typical point sits from the mean. A small stdev means the die is repeatable. A large stdev means the same item is jumping around — bolts, mix, or measurement noise. Cpk uses 3 × stdev as “almost all of the process.”

Cp potential

Spec width ÷ (6 × stdev). This ignores where the mean sits. It only asks whether the process is tight enough to fit between Min and Max if it were perfectly aimed. You need both a min and a max. If Cp is low, no amount of aiming will save you — the process is too noisy. If Cp is high but Cpk is low, you only need to re-center (move the mean).

Cpl vs Min

(Mean − Min) ÷ (3 × stdev). Room toward the low side. A small Cpl means the process is hugging Min — thin risk. If there is no Min spec, Cpl is a dash.

Cpu vs Max

(Max − Mean) ÷ (3 × stdev). Room toward the high side. A small Cpu means the process is hugging Max — thick risk. If there is no Max spec, Cpu is a dash.

Cpk the score

The smaller of Cpl and Cpu. Quality Desk colors this pill:

  • Cpk ≥ 1.33 capable — the process sits inside the spec with margin. This is the usual “good” bar.
  • 1.00–1.33 marginal — passing today, little room. A small shift will make fails.
  • below 1.00 not capable — part of the bell already hangs outside the spec. Expect regular fails.
  • — not enough points, or no spec on that side.

Cpk = 1.00 means the nearest spec is exactly 3 standard deviations away. Cpk = 1.33 means 4 standard deviations — a common plant target. Cpk can never be higher than Cp.

What to do when Cpk is red

Look at mean vs target. If mean is off, adjust the process (die, gap, speed) toward target. If mean is fine but stdev is huge, hunt variation — one T-point drifting, mix changes, or a bad gauge.

What to do when Cp is fine and Cpk is not

The doorway is wide enough; people are walking on one side. Re-center. The T-point SPC charts show which location is pulling the average off.

These stats describe the points on the chart, not a crystal ball. A capable Cpk with n = 5 can flip after the next shift. Read Cpk with the picture, not instead of it.

Centration graph

A map of the die. Each T-point is a thickness around the circle. You are looking down the barrel of the die.

  • Specs on the right come from the Master Sheet for that MSPEC. You can edit them for this view without changing Excel.
  • Previous / Next walks History rows for the current filters.
  • Settings changes how many T-points and bolts are drawn — match the sheet (often 13 points).
  • Show arrow draws the shift from the center of the readings toward the thick side.
  • Series graph on the tools bar overlays many checks at once instead of one hour.

Series centration graph

One picture of many hourly die checks stacked on the same radial map. Use it to see whether the die is drifting, not just whether the last hour passed.

How to open it

  • From SPC charts, pick Item # or MSPEC and a date range, then press Series Centration Graph.
  • From a single centration graph, press Series graph in the tools bar. It uses the same Item / MSPEC and the SPC date range (From defaults to the first of last month).

What you are looking at

  • The green and yellow bands are still the spec window. A check inside the green band is in spec at that T-point.
  • Lines and dots are thinner than the single-check graph so 20 or 50 overlays stay readable.
  • Show checks picks the newest 10, 20, 50, 100, or every check in the From / To range.
  • The dashed average shape is the typical die for those checks. If the newest ring pulls away from it, the process just shifted.

Foam only. Bubble, Garland, P1, and RTS do not use this radial overlay — Bubble and Garland use the Basis Weight SPC chart instead.

Offline HTA

Quality Desk can run with no internet. Double-click the HTA on the shop-floor folder — it does not open a website.

  • Keep quality-desk.hta, qd-check.js, index.html (or centration.html), and Quality AIO.xlsm in the same folder. Do not put the AIO workbook on GitHub.
  • The HTA requires a sign-in. Passwords are hashed in a hidden results/users.dat on this PC. Idle sessions end after one hour.
  • If item/user lists stay empty, delete results/aio-cache.js and click Reload AIO. Better: import ExportAioCsv.bas into Quality AIO and run it so Table114 / Table7 / Table86 / Table97 / Table18 land in aio-csv.
  • Put BubbleSpecs.csv next to the HTA or in aio-csv for COEX/MONO. Do not publish that file.
  • The HTA is the check sheet: look up an item, view specs, submit. Submit stays on the form and clears the check. Each line writes its own file under results/ so S4 does not overwrite S1.
  • Quality Desk reviews those files. Use Reload from disk after a submit. Open Quality Desk only when you want the review window.
  • Chart.js lives in vendor/chart.umd.min.js so Trends does not need a CDN.
